**R290 vs R32** is one of the first technical choices you'll come across when comparing heat pumps. Both are refrigerants: the fluid that carries heat from the outside air into your heating system. They work in the same way but differ on climate impact, the temperatures they reach comfortably and how they're regulated.

## The short answer

For most UK homes, **R290 is the better long-term choice**: a far lower climate impact if it ever leaks, strong performance at higher water temperatures, and no exposure to tightening rules on high-GWP refrigerants. R32 heat pumps are still widely sold and work well, but they sit on the wrong side of where regulation is heading.

## R290 and R32 side by side

| | R290 (propane) | R32 |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Natural refrigerant (hydrocarbon) | Synthetic fluorinated gas (HFC) |
| Global warming potential | 3 | 675 |
| Flammability | Highly flammable (A3) | Mildly flammable (A2L) |
| Typical system type | Monobloc, sealed outdoor unit | Monobloc or split |
| High-temperature performance | Strong, up to around 65–75°C water | Weaker above about 55°C |
| Regulatory outlook | Favoured | Being phased down in the EU |

*GWP over 100 years, as used in F-gas regulations.*

## Climate impact: GWP 3 vs 675

Global warming potential compares a gas's warming effect with carbon dioxide. A GWP of 675 means one kilogram of R32 released into the air warms the planet as much as 675 kilograms of CO₂. R290's GWP of 3 makes the same leak almost irrelevant.

A well-installed heat pump shouldn't leak. But over 15–20 years of service, repairs and eventually disposal, some refrigerant risk is real. With R290, that risk barely matters for the climate.

## Hot water and older radiators

R290 can produce higher water temperatures efficiently. That's useful for:

- **Hot water**, which needs to be stored at 50°C or more and periodically heated to around 60°C to control legionella.
- **Older homes** where some radiators can't be upsized and need a warmer flow on the coldest days.

The Outback range is tested at flow temperatures up to 65°C. At 7°C outside, the Outback 8kW still delivers 8.26 kW at 65°C, with a COP of 2.25. Lower flow temperatures remain far more efficient, so good design still aims for 35–50°C.

## Is R290 safe?

Yes, in a system designed for it. R290 is propane, the same gas used in camping stoves, so it's flammable. That's managed by:

- **Monobloc design.** All the refrigerant stays sealed inside the outdoor unit. Only water runs into your home.
- **Small charge.** A domestic monobloc holds a small amount of refrigerant. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: ilość czynnika R290 w każdym modelu Outback (kg)]]
- **Safety standards** for equipment and installation, including clearances from openings and ignition sources set out in the manual.

## What the rules say: Great Britain vs the EU

- **EU (and Northern Ireland):** from 1 January 2027, new split heat pumps and air conditioners under 12 kW using refrigerants with a GWP of 150 or more can't be placed on the market. That rules out R32 in that category.
- **Great Britain (England, Scotland, Wales):** GB still applies its retained version of the older EU F-gas rules and hasn't adopted the 2024 revision. A consultation on reforming the GB phase-down closed in December 2025. As of October 2026, there is no GB ban on R32 heat pumps.

What that means for you: an R32 heat pump installed today in England or Wales is legal and will be serviceable. But manufacturers and regulators are moving towards low-GWP refrigerants, and R290 already meets that direction.

## Does refrigerant affect running costs?

Less than design does. Both refrigerants can deliver excellent seasonal efficiency. What moves your bill most is correct sizing and a low flow temperature. Outback units achieve a SCOP of up to 5.22 at 35°C flow (MCS data). See [COP vs SCOP](/guides/cop-vs-scop) for what those figures mean.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is R290 better than R32?
For climate impact and future regulation, yes. For everyday efficiency, both can perform well; design matters more.

### Will R32 heat pumps be banned in the UK?
Not in Great Britain as of October 2026. The EU restricts new split units under 12 kW from 2027, and that applies in Northern Ireland.

### Can I service an R290 heat pump with any engineer?
Work on the sealed refrigerant circuit needs an engineer trained for hydrocarbon refrigerants. Routine servicing of the water side doesn't.

### Do R290 heat pumps cost more?
Prices vary by brand more than by refrigerant. Compare full installed quotes, not unit prices.
