The **monobloc vs split heat pump** choice is about where the refrigerant lives. Both are air source heat pumps, both heat radiators and hot water, and both can be very efficient. The difference shapes how the system is installed, how much space it takes up indoors and which refrigerants it can use.

## How a monobloc heat pump works

Everything that handles refrigerant is in **one sealed outdoor unit**: the fan, coil, compressor, valves and the heat exchanger that passes heat to water. Only **water pipes** run from the unit into your home, to the cylinder and radiators.

The Outback range is monobloc.

## How a split heat pump works

The system is **split into two units**. The outdoor unit holds the fan, coil and compressor. **Refrigerant pipes** run into the house to an indoor unit (often called a hydrobox), where the heat passes to water.

## Monobloc vs split at a glance

| | Monobloc | Split |
|---|---|---|
| Refrigerant circuit | Sealed in the outdoor unit at the factory | Completed on site between two units |
| Pipes into the house | Water | Refrigerant |
| Installer qualifications | Heating engineer; no on-site refrigerant work | Also needs F-gas qualified engineer for refrigerant |
| Indoor space | Cylinder and controls only | Indoor unit plus cylinder |
| Freeze risk in outdoor pipes | Yes, needs frost protection | No, refrigerant doesn't freeze |
| Distance from unit to house | Best kept short | Can be longer |
| Low-GWP refrigerant (R290) | Common | Rare in homes |

## Advantages of a monobloc

- **Simpler installation.** No refrigerant is handled on site, which removes a specialist step and a source of leaks.
- **Less indoor space.** No separate indoor unit on the wall.
- **Suits natural refrigerants.** R290 is flammable, so keeping all of it outside in a sealed unit is the safe, standard approach. See [R290 vs R32](/guides/r290-vs-r32).
- **Sealed for life.** A factory-sealed circuit reduces the chance of a refrigerant leak over the unit's life.

## Advantages of a split

- **No freezing risk** in the pipes between the units.
- **Longer runs** between the outdoor unit and the house are easier.
- **Smaller outdoor unit** in some designs.

## Monobloc frost protection

Because water runs outside to the unit, a monobloc needs protection against freezing during a power cut. Installers use one or more of:

- **Insulated pipework** to and from the unit.
- **Anti-freeze valves** that drain water from the outdoor pipes if they get close to freezing.
- **Glycol** in the system, in some designs.
- The heat pump's own **frost protection mode**, which circulates water when it's very cold.

## Which is better for a UK home?

For most homes, **a monobloc** is the simpler and more common choice: easier to install, less space indoors, and the natural fit for low-GWP refrigerants. A split can make sense where the outdoor unit must be far from the house or where there's no practical way to protect outdoor water pipes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a monobloc heat pump less efficient than a split?
Not inherently. Efficiency depends on the unit, design and flow temperature far more than on the layout.

### Does a monobloc heat pump need an F-gas engineer?
Not for installation, because the refrigerant circuit is sealed at the factory. Repairs to the sealed circuit need a suitably qualified engineer.

### Can a monobloc be far from the house?
Keep the run short where possible. Longer water runs lose a little heat and need more frost protection.

### Are split heat pumps being phased out?
In the EU, new split heat pumps under 12 kW using refrigerants with a GWP of 150 or more are banned from 2027. Great Britain hasn't adopted that rule as of October 2026.
