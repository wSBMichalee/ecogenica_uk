Good news first: **heat pump planning permission** isn't needed for most air source heat pumps on houses in England. They count as *permitted development* as long as they meet a short list of conditions. Those conditions were relaxed on 29 May 2025, so advice written before then is out of date.

## The permitted development conditions for houses

An air source heat pump on a house in England is permitted development if:

- **Size:** the outdoor unit, including any housing, is no more than **1.5 m³**.
- **Number of units:** a **detached** house can have up to **two** heat pumps; other houses **one**.
- **Noise:** the installation meets the **MCS 020** noise standard: no more than **37 dB** at an assessment point 1 metre outside the nearest neighbouring habitable room window or door.
- **Not on a pitched roof.** On a flat roof, every part of the unit must be at least 1 metre from the roof edge.
- **Not on a wall facing a road above ground floor level.**
- **It's used for heating** (it may also cool, but not be used only for cooling).

There's **no longer a minimum distance from the boundary**. Units should still be placed to minimise their effect on how the property looks and on neighbours.

### How big are Outback units?

| Model | Dimensions (L × W × H) | Volume |
|---|---|---|
| Outback 5kW | 1,045 × 400 × 805 mm | 0.34 m³ |
| Outback 8kW | 1,205 × 475 × 845 mm | 0.48 m³ |
| Outback 11kW | 1,205 × 475 × 1,015 mm | 0.58 m³ |
| Outback 16kW | 1,205 × 475 × 1,435 mm | 0.82 m³ |

All four are comfortably within the 1.5 m³ limit.

## When you do need permission or consent

- **Listed buildings and scheduled monuments.** Permitted development doesn't apply within their grounds. You'll need listed building consent and possibly planning permission.
- **Conservation areas and World Heritage Sites.** Units can't go on a wall or roof facing a road, or nearer to a road than any part of the house.
- **Article 4 directions** or planning conditions on your property can remove permitted development rights. Your council can tell you.
- **Flats.** The size limit is 0.6 m³, and the block must consist wholly of flats. You'll also need the freeholder's agreement.
- **More units than allowed**, or a unit that fails the noise test.

## The noise assessment, explained

Your installer completes the MCS 020 calculation as part of the design. It uses the unit's sound level, the distance to the neighbour's window, any solid barriers in between and nearby reflecting surfaces. If the result is above 37 dB, the options are to move the unit, add a barrier, choose a quieter model or apply for planning permission.

## Building Regulations still apply

Permitted development covers planning only. The installation must still comply with Building Regulations. MCS-certified installers notify the work through a competent person scheme, so you don't apply separately.

## Wales and Scotland

The rules above are for **England**. Wales and Scotland have their own permitted development rules, which differ in places. Check with your installer or local planning authority.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need planning permission for an air source heat pump?
On most houses in England, no, as long as it meets the permitted development conditions above.

### How close to my neighbour's boundary can a heat pump be?
There's no fixed minimum distance since May 2025. The unit must still pass the 37 dB noise limit at the neighbour's window.

### Can I install two heat pumps?
On a detached house, yes, as permitted development. On semi-detached and terraced houses, only one is permitted development.

### Do I need to tell my neighbours?
It isn't a legal requirement for permitted development, but it's a good idea to talk to them before installation.
