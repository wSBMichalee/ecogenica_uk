A heat pump that's well designed but badly set up can cost noticeably more to run than it should. The good news: a handful of **heat pump settings** make most of the difference, and you can check them yourself in a few minutes.

> **[callout: info]** Your installer sets these at commissioning. Change them gradually, a step at a time, and give each change a few days before judging it.

## 1. Flow temperature: as low as keeps you comfortable

Flow temperature is how hot the water leaving the heat pump is. Lower flow temperatures mean higher efficiency. From Outback 8kW test data at 2°C outside:

| Flow temperature | COP |
|---|---|
| 35°C | 3.54 |
| 45°C | 2.85 |
| 55°C | 2.52 |

Going from 55°C to 45°C saves about 12% of the electricity for the same heat; going to 35°C saves about 29%. The limit is comfort: if rooms don't reach temperature on cold days, the flow is too low for your radiators.

## 2. Weather compensation: leave it on

Weather compensation adjusts the flow temperature to the outside temperature using a curve: hotter on cold days, cooler on mild ones. Most of the heating season is mild, so this saves a lot.

- **If rooms are too cold only in cold weather:** steepen the curve slightly.
- **If rooms are too warm in mild weather:** lower the curve slightly.
- Adjust in small steps. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: jak zmienić krzywą pogodową w sterowniku/aplikacji Outback — kroki i zrzuty]]

## 3. Run steadily, avoid big setbacks

Gas boilers suit short, hot bursts. Heat pumps don't. They're most efficient running for long periods at low output.

- **Keep a steady target temperature** most of the day.
- **Small night setbacks** of 1–2°C are usually fine.
- **Avoid switching off for hours** and then demanding fast recovery. The recovery runs at a high flow temperature and can cost more than you saved. See [should you leave a heat pump on all the time?](/guides/leave-heat-pump-on-all-the-time)

## 4. Hot water: schedule it, don't overheat it

- **Schedule cylinder heating** for times you'll use it, ideally in cheap tariff windows if you're on a time-of-use tariff.
- **Store at around 45–50°C** for everyday use. Higher temperatures cost more.
- **Legionella cycle:** the system heats the cylinder to around 60°C periodically. Keep it, but once a week is typical. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: ustawienie cyklu antylegionella w Outback]]

## 5. Thermostats and radiator valves

- **Use one main thermostat** (or the heat pump's own room sensor) rather than many competing controls.
- **Keep thermostatic radiator valves (TRVs) open** in rooms you use, so water can flow. A system with most valves closed can't run efficiently.
- **Turn down rooms you don't use** a little, rather than closing them off completely.

## Check how efficient your heat pump is

The Outback app shows energy use. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: czy aplikacja pokazuje ciepło wyprodukowane / COP]] If you can see both heat produced and electricity used over a week, divide one by the other: that's your real-world efficiency. Compare it across weeks with similar weather after each change.

## Settings to leave to your installer

- **Pump speed and system flow rate.**
- **Maximum flow temperature limits.**
- **Defrost and frost protection settings.**

Changing these can affect efficiency, comfort and your warranty.

## Frequently asked questions

### What's the best flow temperature for a heat pump?
The lowest that keeps your home comfortable on cold days. For many well-designed systems that's 35–45°C.

### Should I use the room thermostat or weather compensation?
Weather compensation does the main work; a room sensor can fine-tune it. Your installer will set up the combination that suits your system.

### Why are my radiators only warm, not hot?
That's normal with a heat pump. They run cooler for longer. If rooms reach temperature, the system is working as designed.

### Can I use the app to turn the heating off when I'm out?
For a few hours, a small setback is better than off. For holidays, use holiday or frost-protection mode.
