On a cold, damp morning your heat pump may stop, hiss, and release a cloud of what looks like smoke. That's **heat pump defrost** at work, and it's completely normal. Here's what's happening and how to tell normal defrosting from a problem.

## Why do heat pumps frost up?

An air source heat pump pulls heat out of the outside air by passing it over a cold coil (the evaporator). The coil runs colder than the air around it. When the air is cold and humid, moisture freezes on the coil, just like frost on a car windscreen.

Frost is most likely when it's **around 0°C to 5°C with high humidity**: very typical of a British winter. In very cold, dry weather there's less moisture in the air, so frost actually builds more slowly.

A layer of frost blocks airflow and reduces efficiency, so the heat pump clears it regularly.

## What does defrost mode do?

1. **The controller detects frost** from temperature sensors on the coil.
2. **The cycle reverses briefly.** A four-way valve sends warm refrigerant to the outdoor coil instead of to your home.
3. **The frost melts** and drains away from the base of the unit.
4. **Normal heating resumes**, usually within a few minutes. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: typowy czas cyklu defrost Outback]]

During defrost the fan often stops, so the unit sounds different. Afterwards you may notice a short whoosh as it restarts.

## Steam or smoke?

**Steam.** When warm meltwater meets cold air, it evaporates into a visible plume. It can look dramatic, especially in still air, and it disappears within a minute or two. A heat pump doesn't burn anything, so it can't produce smoke.

## Does defrosting waste energy?

It uses a little, and it's built into the efficiency figures. Tests to BS EN 14511 at 2°C and below include defrost cycles. That's part of why COP is lower at 2°C (2.85 for the Outback 8kW at 45°C flow) than at 7°C (3.45).

## What's normal and what isn't

| Normal | Call support |
|---|---|
| Steam plume for a minute or two | Ice that keeps building up and never clears |
| A thin layer of frost between cycles | The coil fully encased in ice |
| Water dripping from the base | Ice building up underneath the unit and blocking drainage |
| Fan stopping during defrost | An error code on the controller or app |
| A few defrosts an hour in damp, cold weather | Defrosting constantly with little heat reaching the house |

## How to help your heat pump in winter

- **Keep the base and drain clear** so meltwater can run away. A frozen puddle under the unit can build up into the coil.
- **Clear snow** from around and on top of the unit, gently and without tools on the fins.
- **Don't pour hot water** over the coil. It can damage the fins and refreezes quickly.
- **Don't cover it.** Covers block airflow.
- **Check the installation height.** Units should sit above typical snow levels on feet or a bracket.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often does a heat pump defrost?
It depends on temperature and humidity. In cold, damp weather it can be a few times an hour; in dry or mild weather, rarely.

### Will my house get cold during defrost?
No. Defrost cycles are short and the heating system holds enough warmth to cover them.

### Why is my heat pump steaming when it's not frosty?
On damp days the coil can still collect a little ice even if surfaces elsewhere look clear. Short steam plumes are normal.

### Is it safe to clear ice myself?
Clear snow and ice from around the base gently. Leave the coil to the defrost cycle, and call support if ice doesn't clear.
