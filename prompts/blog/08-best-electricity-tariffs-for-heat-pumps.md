The **best electricity tariff for a heat pump** is the one whose cheap hours match how your home uses heat. Since a heat pump becomes most of your electricity use, the tariff matters almost as much as the heat pump itself. This guide explains how heat pump tariffs work and how to compare them. We don't take commission from any supplier.

## Standard tariff vs heat pump tariff

- **Standard variable tariff (price cap):** one unit rate all day. From October to December 2026 the average cap is **26.32p per kWh** for electricity paid by Direct Debit, with VAT removed from household electricity until 31 March 2027.
- **Heat pump or time-of-use tariff:** cheaper rates in set windows, a standard rate the rest of the day and, on some tariffs, a higher peak rate in the early evening.

On a standard tariff, the heat pump's running cost is simply its electricity use times the unit rate. On a time-of-use tariff, *when* it runs matters.

## How a heat pump fits a time-of-use day

A heat pump with a hot water cylinder is well suited to cheap windows:

- **Hot water:** heat the cylinder in an overnight or afternoon cheap window. A well-insulated cylinder holds its heat for the evening.
- **Pre-heating:** let the house warm a little more in the afternoon cheap window, then coast through an evening peak.
- **Weather compensation** keeps running steadily the rest of the time, which is still efficient.

What doesn't work: switching the heating off for the peak and turning it on hard afterwards. A big recovery at a high flow temperature costs more than you save.

## How to compare heat pump tariffs

1. **Estimate your yearly heat pump electricity.** A heat loss survey or your installer's estimate gives this. For example, a home needing 8,550 kWh of heat at a seasonal efficiency of 3.1 uses about 2,750 kWh.
2. **Split it across the day.** Roughly what share falls in each window? Hot water can go almost entirely into cheap hours; space heating is spread out.
3. **Add your other household electricity** in the same way.
4. **Price each window** with the tariff's current rates and add the standing charge.
5. **Compare the totals**, not the headline cheap rate.

> **[callout: tip]** If you also have solar panels or a home battery, a tariff with an afternoon cheap window or a good export rate can change the result. See [heat pumps and solar panels](/guides/heat-pump-and-solar-panels).

## Do you need a smart meter?

For time-of-use tariffs, yes. The supplier needs half-hourly readings to charge different rates by time of day.

## When a standard tariff is fine

If you can't shift much use into cheap windows, for example you're home all day with steady heating and no cylinder scheduling, a tariff with a high peak rate may not help. Run the comparison above before switching.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to buy my heat pump from the supplier to get their tariff?
For the tariffs listed above, the providers' published terms do not require a heat pump bought from them. Check current eligibility before switching.

### Can I control my Outback heat pump to use cheap hours?
Yes. The Outback app supports schedules for heating and hot water. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: funkcje harmonogramu w aplikacji Outback]]

### How often should I review my tariff?
At least once a year and whenever Ofgem changes the price cap (every quarter).

### Does the electricity VAT cut affect heat pump tariffs?
VAT has been removed from household electricity from 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, which lowers all electricity tariffs, including heat pump tariffs.
