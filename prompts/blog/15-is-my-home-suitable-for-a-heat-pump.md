**Is my home suitable for a heat pump?** For most UK homes, yes. Detached, semi-detached, terraced, older and newer homes all run on heat pumps today. What changes from home to home is how much work is needed around the heat pump: radiators, a cylinder, sometimes insulation.

Use these ten checks to see where your home stands before a surveyor visits.

## 1. Is there outside space for the unit?

An air source heat pump sits outside, usually against a wall or on feet on a solid base. It needs clear air around it to work well.

- **Outback sizes:** from 1,045 × 400 × 805 mm (5 kW) up to 1,205 × 475 × 1,435 mm (16 kW).
- **Airflow:** leave space in front and behind as set out in the installation manual. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: odstępy montażowe z manuala Outback]]
- **Distance to pipe runs:** closer to where the heating pipes enter the house means less disruption.

A side return, a back wall or a corner of the garden usually works. See [where to put an air source heat pump](/guides/where-to-put-an-air-source-heat-pump).

## 2. Is there room for a hot water cylinder?

Heat pumps heat water into a stored cylinder rather than on demand like a combi boiler. You'll need a space roughly the size of an airing cupboard.

If you have a combi and no cupboard, there are options: a slimline cylinder, a cupboard built into a loft or garage, or an outdoor all-in-one unit. Our upcoming [Wallaroo](/heat-pumps/wallaroo) keeps the cylinder outside entirely.

## 3. How well does the house keep heat in?

Insulation decides how much heat you need, and that decides the size and running cost of the heat pump. It doesn't decide whether a heat pump can work.

- Loft insulation, cavity walls, double glazing and draught-proofing all lower your bills.
- **You don't need insulation to get the grant.** The Boiler Upgrade Scheme dropped the loft and cavity wall insulation requirement in 2024.
- Quick wins like draught-proofing doors and topping up loft insulation are worth doing either way.

## 4. Are your radiators big enough?

Heat pumps are most efficient at low flow temperatures, around 35–50°C, compared with 70°C or more from a typical gas boiler. Lower water temperature means radiators need more surface area to give the same heat.

Some of your radiators will probably be fine. A few, often in living rooms and rooms with large windows, may need upsizing. The survey checks each room. More in [do you need new radiators for a heat pump?](/guides/do-you-need-new-radiators-for-a-heat-pump)

## 5. What's the pipework like?

Very narrow microbore pipes (8–10 mm) can limit flow to radiators. They don't always need replacing, but the installer will check flow rates. Standard 15 mm pipework is usually fine.

## 6. Is your electrical supply suitable?

A heat pump needs its own circuit. The installer notifies your network operator before connecting.

- **Single phase** (most homes): suits the Outback 5, 8 and 11 kW.
- **Three phase**: the Outback 16 kW needs a three-phase supply. Most homes don't have one, so the survey checks this early for larger properties.

## 7. What type of property is it?

- **Houses and bungalows:** usually straightforward.
- **Flats:** possible, but you'll need the freeholder's permission and planning rules are tighter.
- **Listed buildings and conservation areas:** possible with consent. See [planning permission rules](/guides/heat-pump-planning-permission-rules).

## 8. Will you need planning permission?

Most houses in England don't. Since May 2025, a heat pump is permitted development if it meets the size, siting and noise conditions, and the old 1-metre-from-the-boundary rule is gone. Every Outback model is well under the 1.5 m³ size limit. Wales and Scotland have their own rules.

## 9. Do you have a valid EPC?

For the Boiler Upgrade Scheme, the property needs an Energy Performance Certificate from the last 10 years. If yours is older or missing, an assessor can produce one, usually within a few days.

## 10. Does it add up after the grant?

The Boiler Upgrade Scheme takes £7,500 off an air source heat pump in England and Wales, or £9,000 for homes heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas until 31 March 2027. Installation is zero-rated for VAT until the same date.

## What a survey checks that you can't

A checklist gets you most of the way. A survey adds:

- **Room-by-room heat loss**, which sets the heat pump size and radiator upgrades.
- **Flow temperature design**, which sets your running costs.
- **Noise assessment** at your neighbours' windows, needed for permitted development.
- **Grant eligibility**, confirmed before you see a price.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a heat pump work in an old house?
Yes. Older homes lose more heat, so they may need a larger unit or more radiator upgrades. A heat loss survey shows exactly what's needed.

### Do I need underfloor heating for a heat pump?
No. Most heat pumps in UK homes run on radiators. Underfloor heating works well with heat pumps but isn't required.

### Can I get a heat pump if I have a combi boiler?
Yes. You'll need space for a hot water cylinder, or an outdoor all-in-one unit.
