**Heat pump running costs in winter** are higher than in summer for two reasons: your home needs more heat, and the heat pump works a little harder to take heat from colder air. Both are predictable. Below is a month-by-month example with every assumption shown, so you can swap in your own numbers.

## Why do heat pump bills rise in winter?

Because demand rises and efficiency dips at the same time.

- **More heat needed.** A typical UK home needs several times more heat in January than in October.
- **Lower efficiency.** A heat pump's COP (units of heat out per unit of electricity in) falls as the outside air gets colder and as the water it sends to your radiators gets hotter.

The drop is gradual, not a cliff. Here is what the test data for the Outback 8kW shows at a 45°C flow temperature, which suits many homes with upgraded radiators:

| Outside air | Heat output | COP |
|---|---|---|
| 7°C | 9.73 kW | 3.45 |
| 2°C | 9.72 kW | 2.85 |
| 0°C | 9.52 kW | 2.82 |
| −7°C | 7.33 kW | 2.41 |

*Outback 8kW (ECO-ZR03FC), tested to BS EN 14511, 45°C flow. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: wartości z karty MCS]]*

Even at −7°C, every unit of electricity still delivers about 2.4 units of heat. An electric heater delivers 1.

## A month-by-month example

This is an **illustrative example**, not a quote. The assumptions:

- **Heat demand: 8,550 kWh a year** for heating and hot water. That's what a home on Ofgem's typical gas use (9,500 kWh a year, the value used since July 2026) gets from a 90% efficient boiler.
- **Monthly split** follows a typical UK heating season, with hot water through the summer.
- **COP by month** taken from the Outback 8kW test points above, between 3.0 in the coldest months and 3.4 in spring and autumn.
- **Electricity at 26.32p per kWh**, the average price cap unit rate for October to December 2026. Standing charge not included.

| Month | Heat needed (kWh) | Assumed COP | Electricity (kWh) | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jan | 1,282 | 3.0 | 428 | £113 |
| Feb | 1,112 | 3.0 | 370 | £98 |
| Mar | 940 | 3.2 | 294 | £77 |
| Apr | 598 | 3.4 | 176 | £46 |
| May | 342 | 3.0 | 114 | £30 |
| Jun | 256 | 3.0 | 86 | £23 |
| Jul | 256 | 3.0 | 86 | £23 |
| Aug | 256 | 3.0 | 86 | £23 |
| Sep | 342 | 3.0 | 114 | £30 |
| Oct | 684 | 3.4 | 201 | £53 |
| Nov | 1,112 | 3.2 | 347 | £91 |
| Dec | 1,368 | 3.0 | 456 | £120 |
| **Year** | **8,550** | **3.1 average** | **2,757** | **£726** |

*Summer months use COP 3.0 because most of the work is hot water at a higher temperature.*

November to February accounts for about **£421**, or close to 60% of the year. That's why winter bills feel high even when the annual figure compares well with gas or oil.

## How does that compare with gas and oil?

For the same 8,550 kWh of heat, at October 2026 prices:

| Heating | Cost per kWh of heat | Example year |
|---|---|---|
| Heat pump, standard price cap rate, COP 3.1 | about 8.5p | £726 |
| Gas boiler, 90% efficient | about 8.9p | £757 + £108 gas standing charge |
| Oil boiler, 90% efficient (117.9p/litre) | about 12.7p | £1,082 |

On the standard rate, a heat pump is roughly level with gas and well ahead of oil. On a heat pump tariff with cheaper hours, the gap widens. See [the best electricity tariffs for heat pumps](/guides/best-electricity-tariffs-for-heat-pumps).

## What changes the winter figure?

- **Flow temperature.** The biggest lever. Every few degrees lower raises COP. At −7°C, the Outback 8kW's COP is 2.88 at 35°C flow and 2.02 at 55°C.
- **Your tariff.** Time-of-use heat pump tariffs can cut the price of most of your winter electricity.
- **Insulation and draughts.** Less heat lost means less heat to make.
- **Settings.** Weather compensation and steady running beat on/off bursts. See [heat pump settings that cut your bills](/guides/heat-pump-settings-to-save-money).
- **Correct sizing.** An undersized unit leans on backup heating on cold days, which is expensive.

## How to keep winter costs down

1. **Let weather compensation do its job.** It lowers the flow temperature automatically on milder days.
2. **Run it steadily.** Big overnight setbacks force the pump to work hard (and hot) in the morning.
3. **Heat hot water in cheap hours** if you're on a time-of-use tariff.
4. **Keep the outdoor unit clear** of leaves and snow so it can breathe and defrost properly.
5. **Book the annual service.** It keeps efficiency up and your warranty valid.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do heat pumps stop working in very cold weather?
No. Outback units are rated to operate down to −15°C. Output and efficiency fall in deep cold, which is why the system is sized for your home's coldest design day.

### Why is my heat pump bill higher than my old gas bill in January?
Because you were paying for gas separately before, your electricity bill now carries all of your heating. Compare the total across both fuels over a full year.

### Does the price cap figure include VAT?
From 1 October 2026 to 31 March 2027, the government has removed VAT from household electricity, and the price cap figure reflects that. Gas still includes 5% VAT.

### Is the example the same as my bill will be?
No. It shows the method. Your heat demand, flow temperature and tariff will change the result. A heat loss survey gives you a reliable figure.
