**Heat pump noise** is one of the most common worries we hear, mostly from people picturing an air conditioning unit rattling outside a hotel. A modern air source heat pump makes a steady, low hum from its fan and compressor. Sited well, most neighbours never notice it.

## What does a heat pump sound like?

A soft, constant whoosh from the fan, with a low hum from the compressor underneath. It gets a little louder when the heat pump is working hard on a cold day and quieter when demand is low, because inverter compressors like the Outback's speed up and slow down instead of switching fully on and off.

Two sounds people sometimes don't expect:

- **Defrost.** A few times a day in cold, damp weather, the unit pauses, reverses briefly and may release a puff of steam with a hiss. That's normal. See [heat pump defrost mode](/guides/heat-pump-defrost-mode).
- **Start-up.** A short change in tone as the compressor ramps up.

## Reading the decibels on a datasheet

Datasheets list a noise figure in dB(A). For the Outback range:

| Outback 8kW | 60.54 dB(A) |
| Outback 11kW | 63.04 dB(A) |
| Outback 16kW | 68.26 dB(A) |

Three things to know about decibels:

- **They're logarithmic.** +3 dB is double the sound energy. +10 dB sounds roughly twice as loud.
- **Distance matters a lot.** In open air, sound drops by about 6 dB each time you double the distance from the source.
- **Hard surfaces add noise.** A unit tucked into a corner between two walls reflects sound and can be several decibels louder than the same unit on an open wall.

So a figure measured at the unit tells you very little on its own. What matters is the level where people are: at your neighbour's window and in your own garden.

## The planning limit: 37 dB at the neighbour's window

In England, a heat pump is permitted development (no planning application) only if it meets the noise standard set by MCS 020. The installer calculates the noise at an assessment position **1 metre outside the nearest habitable room window or door of a neighbouring home**. The current method, MCS 020 a), is designed to confirm a limit of **37 dB** at that point.

For a sense of scale, 37 dB is a quiet level, somewhere around the hum of a quiet room. The calculation takes into account the unit's sound level, distance, any barriers in between and reflecting surfaces nearby.

If the calculation shows the limit is exceeded, the installer can move the unit, add a barrier or apply for planning permission instead.

> **[callout: info]** Wales and Scotland have their own permitted development rules. Your installer will check the rules that apply to your address.

## How to keep heat pump noise down

1. **Site it away from bedroom windows**, yours and your neighbours'.
2. **Avoid tight corners and narrow passages** that reflect and channel sound.
3. **Use anti-vibration feet or brackets** and a solid, level base. Vibration through a thin wall is a common cause of complaints.
4. **Consider a barrier.** A solid fence or wall between the unit and the neighbour's window can cut the level noticeably. It must not block airflow.
5. **Size it correctly.** An oversized unit cycles more; an undersized one runs flat out on cold days. A proper heat loss calculation avoids both.
6. **Use quiet mode** for night-time if your controls offer it. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: czy sterownik Outback ma tryb cichy/nocny]]
7. **Keep it serviced.** A worn fan bearing or loose panel adds noise.

## Will my neighbours complain?

Rarely, when the unit meets the 37 dB limit and is sited with care. Most complaints come from units placed close to a bedroom window, on a wall that carries vibration, or in a spot that reflects sound. A short conversation with your neighbour before installation also goes a long way.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are heat pumps noisier than gas boilers?
They're different. A gas boiler is inside and noisy close up; a heat pump is outside with a steady hum. Inside the house, most people hear nothing from a heat pump.

### Are heat pumps loud at night?
They can run at night, but usually at lower output, so they're quieter. Siting away from bedrooms matters most.

### Does a bigger heat pump make more noise?
Larger units have higher datasheet figures, as the Outback table shows. A correctly sized unit for your home matters more than picking the smallest.

### Can I put a heat pump in front of my house?
In England, not on a wall above ground floor level that faces a road, and in conservation areas not on a wall facing a road at all. See [planning permission rules](/guides/heat-pump-planning-permission-rules).
