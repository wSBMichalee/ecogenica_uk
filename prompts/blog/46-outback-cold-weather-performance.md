"Do heat pumps work when it's freezing?" is the question we're asked most. The honest answer is in the lab data. Here is the **heat pump performance in cold weather** for every Outback model, tested to the European standard BS EN 14511, and what it means for a British winter.

## How to read the numbers

Two figures matter at each temperature:

- **Heat output (kW):** how much heat the unit delivers.
- **COP (coefficient of performance):** units of heat delivered per unit of electricity used. A COP of 3 means 3 kWh of heat for 1 kWh of electricity.

Both depend on two temperatures: the **outside air** and the **flow temperature** (how hot the water going to your radiators is).

## Outback performance at 35°C flow

The temperature well-designed systems with larger radiators or underfloor heating aim for.

|---|---|---|---|
| Outback 5kW | 6.65 kW · COP 4.68 | 5.85 kW · COP 3.59 | 4.74 kW · COP 2.98 |
| Outback 8kW | 10.64 kW · COP 4.36 | 10.67 kW · COP 3.54 | 7.95 kW · COP 2.88 |
| Outback 11kW | 13.78 kW · COP 4.29 | 14.50 kW · COP 3.44 | 11.36 kW · COP 2.76 |
| Outback 16kW | 20.69 kW · COP 4.36 | 19.32 kW · COP 3.82 | 16.11 kW · COP 3.05 |

*Tested to BS EN 14511. Output at maximum compressor speed. Outback units are rated to operate from −15°C to +40°C outside.*

## What the data tells you

**1. Heat pumps keep working well below freezing.** At −7°C with a 35°C flow, every model delivers about 2.8 to 3.1 times more heat than the electricity it uses; at 55°C flow, about 1.9 to 2.3 times. An electric heater manages 1.

**2. Flow temperature matters more than the weather.** At −7°C, the Outback 8kW's COP is 2.88 at 35°C flow and 2.02 at 55°C. Running cooler saves around 30% of the electricity for the same heat. That's why heat pump installers upsize some radiators. See [do you need new radiators for a heat pump?](/guides/do-you-need-new-radiators-for-a-heat-pump)

**3. Output drops in deep cold, so sizing matters.** The Outback 8kW gives 10.64 kW at 7°C but 7.95 kW at −7°C (35°C flow). Your installer sizes the system so the output at your area's design temperature covers your home's heat loss on that day.

**4. The coldest days are rare.** Most of a UK heating season is spent between about 0°C and 10°C, where efficiency is much higher than at −7°C. That's why seasonal efficiency (SCOP) is higher than the cold-day COP. See [COP vs SCOP](/guides/cop-vs-scop).

## How cold does it get where you live?

Installers use a **design outdoor temperature** for your location: the cold-day temperature the system must cope with. For much of England it is a few degrees below freezing. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: przykładowe temperatury projektowe CIBSE/MCS dla Leicestershire i okolic — źródło]]

## Defrosting in cold, damp weather

Between about 0°C and 5°C in humid air, frost forms on the outdoor coil and the unit defrosts itself automatically. The test figures at 2°C and below already include this. More in [heat pump defrost mode](/guides/heat-pump-defrost-mode).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do heat pumps work in the UK winter?
Yes. UK winters are mild by heat pump standards. The Outback range is rated down to −15°C, and at −7°C with a 35°C flow every model has a COP of about 2.8 or higher.

### Do I need a backup heater?
A correctly sized heat pump covers your home on the design day. Some cylinders have an immersion heater for legionella protection or emergencies.

### Why does my heat pump run longer when it's cold?
It's matching higher heat demand with steady, lower-power running. Longer running at low output is more efficient than short bursts.

### What's the lowest temperature an Outback works at?
The operating range is −15°C to +40°C outside air.
