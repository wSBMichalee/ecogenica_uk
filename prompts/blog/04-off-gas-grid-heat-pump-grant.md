If your home is heated by oil or LPG and isn't connected to mains gas, the **off gas grid heat pump grant** is worth £9,000 towards an air source heat pump. It runs until 31 March 2027. After that, the extra money is due to end and the grant drops back to £7,500.

This guide explains who qualifies, how the money reaches you and what to do now so you don't miss the deadline.

## How much is the grant for oil and LPG homes?

**£9,000 for an air source or ground source heat pump.** The Boiler Upgrade Scheme normally pays £7,500. Until March 2027, homes heated by oil or LPG with no mains gas connection get an extra £1,500 on top.

| Your current heating | Grant for an air source heat pump |
|---|---|
| Mains gas boiler | £7,500 |
| Oil boiler, no mains gas connection | £9,000 (until 31 March 2027) |
| LPG boiler, no mains gas connection | £9,000 (until 31 March 2027) |
| Electric heating (e.g. storage heaters) | £7,500 |

*Grant amounts for England and Wales, checked on GOV.UK and the Energy Saving Trust on 9 October 2026.*

The uplift exists because off-gas homes usually pay the most to heat and have the most to gain from switching. Heating oil and LPG are bought in deliveries, prices swing with the market, and both need a tank on your property.

## Who qualifies for the £9,000 grant?

You're likely to qualify if all of these apply:

- **The property is in England or Wales.** Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own schemes.
- **You own it.** Owner-occupiers, private landlords, second homes and small businesses can apply. Social housing can't.
- **It's currently heated by oil or LPG** and has **no mains gas connection**.
- **It has a valid EPC** issued in the last 10 years. Your installer checks this.
- **It hasn't already had government funding** for a heat pump or biomass boiler.
- **It isn't a new build still under construction.** A finished new build with a fossil fuel boiler may qualify.

Since 2024, you no longer need loft or cavity wall insulation to qualify, even if your EPC recommends it. Insulation still helps your running costs. It just isn't a condition of the grant.

> **[callout: info]** Hybrid systems, where a heat pump works alongside an oil or gas boiler, are not eligible. The grant is for replacing fossil fuel heating completely.

## How does the grant reach you?

You never handle the money. The process is installer-led:

1. **An MCS-certified installer surveys your home** and designs the system.
2. **They apply to Ofgem on your behalf** for a grant voucher.
3. **Ofgem may contact you** to confirm the installer is acting for you.
4. **The grant is deducted from your quote.** You see it on the quotation and the invoice.
5. **The installer commissions the heat pump**, then claims the grant from Ofgem.

## The deadline: why you should start now

The £1,500 uplift is available until **31 March 2027**. Two timings matter:

- **The application.** Your installer applies to Ofgem after the survey and design. Allow a few weeks from first contact to application. [[DO POTWIERDZENIA: typowy czas od zapytania do złożenia wniosku w Ecogenica]]
- **The 120-day window.** Once the voucher is issued, the heat pump must be commissioned within 120 days.

Installers get busy in late winter, when many oil and LPG homes are thinking about their next delivery. If you want the £9,000 rate, aim to have your survey done by early 2027 rather than in March.

> **[callout: warning]** Check with your installer whether the 31 March 2027 date applies to the application or the installation. GOV.UK describes the uplift as available "until March 2027". Plan as if everything needs to be in by then.

## What will it cost after the grant?

That depends on your home's heat loss, the radiators you need and the size of heat pump. A heat pump sized from a proper room-by-room calculation is the only way to get a fair price.

What you can rely on:

- **The grant comes straight off the total**, so you only pay the difference.
- **No VAT on the installation.** Air source heat pump installations in Great Britain are zero-rated for VAT until 31 March 2027.

## Will a heat pump cost less to run than oil or LPG?

In most cases, yes. At October 2026 prices, heat from an oil boiler costs roughly 12.7p per kWh, while a heat pump on the standard price cap rate delivers heat for around 7.5p per kWh at a seasonal efficiency of 3.5. LPG sits in between or above, depending on what you pay per litre.

## Is an off-gas home suitable for a heat pump?

Most are. Rural and older homes often have more outdoor space, which makes siting the unit easier. The things to check are the same as for any home: heat loss, radiator sizes, space for a hot water cylinder and your electrical supply. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get £9,000 if I have mains gas but heat with oil?
The uplift is for properties heated by oil or LPG **with no mains gas connection**. If gas is connected at the property, expect the standard £7,500.

### Does the £9,000 grant apply in Scotland?
No. The Boiler Upgrade Scheme covers England and Wales. Scotland has Home Energy Scotland grants and loans.

### Can landlords get the £9,000 grant?
Yes, private landlords who own the property can apply through an installer. Social housing is excluded.

### What happens after 31 March 2027?
The uplift is due to end, so off-gas homes would get the standard £7,500. Grant rules can change, so check the current amount with your installer.

### Do I need to remove my oil tank?
Not for the grant. Most people decommission or remove it once the heat pump is running. Ask for it to be included in your quote if you want it gone.
